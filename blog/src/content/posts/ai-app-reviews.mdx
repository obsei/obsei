I pointed obsei 1.1 at the App Store reviews of ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini in eight countries:
the US, UK, India, Brazil, Germany, France, Japan and Korea. It pulled the 100 most recent reviews
for each app and country, 2,400 in all, in English, Portuguese, German, French, Japanese, Korean
and a few others. Every review was redacted as it
arrived, then labelled by a model running on my laptop. Nothing was sent to an LLM, and the run
cost nothing.

This post covers what came out, how far to trust it, and what didn't work.

## Read this first

- **This is not a ranking of the apps.** Apple's public feed returns only the newest reviews. Busy
  apps fill 100 reviews in days, quieter ones take weeks: the ChatGPT reviews span 9 to
  14 October, Gemini's 10 to 14 October, and Claude's 9 August to 14 October. Recent reviews also
  skew more negative than an app's lifetime rating. Star mixes from different time windows don't
  compare.
- **The labels come from a small zero-shot model**, [bge-m3-zeroshot-v2.0](https://huggingface.co/MoritzLaurer/bge-m3-zeroshot-v2.0),
  obsei's default. Below I check it against the stars and by reading samples.
- **Every quote is from the stored, redacted text.** Translations are mine.

## The run

The whole configuration is three App Store sources and one classifier with my own questions:
intent, sentiment, and what the review is mainly about (answers, limits, pricing, account, speech,
images, reliability, refusals or other). It's [at the end](#reproduce-it).

On an Apple M2, labelling the 2,400 reviews took 26.5 minutes on the GPU, and grouping them into
themes took 83 seconds.

| App | Reviews | 1–2 stars | Dates covered |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ChatGPT | 800 | 158 (20%) | 9–14 Oct |
| Claude | 800 | 339 (42%) | 9 Aug–14 Oct |
| Gemini | 800 | 87 (11%) | 10–14 Oct |

## What the critical reviews are about

For each 1–2 star review, the model picks the area it is mainly about. I count only answers it
gave with at least 0.4 confidence, the cutoff `obsei init` uses for this model. That leaves about
a third of the reviews; the rest go to a person to read.

| App | Most common confident areas (1–2 stars) |
| --- | --- |
| ChatGPT | reliability 18, limits 11, answers 10, refusals 8 |
| Claude | refusals 27, limits 22, reliability 20, answers 16, account 14 |
| Gemini | answers 5, reliability 3 (82% fell below the cutoff) |

A few of them, as stored:

- ChatGPT, Germany: *"die App ist fürchterlich unzuverlässig. Ständig Verbindungsprobleme"* (the
  app is terribly unreliable, constant connection problems).
- Claude, Germany: *"Man stellt eine aufwendige Frage und gleich ist das Limit erreicht"* (you ask
  one involved question and the limit is reached).
- Gemini, Germany: *"Anfangs fand ich Gemini wirklich top und die Antwort waren eindeutig
  präziser"* (at first I found Gemini really good and the answers were clearly more precise).

### One issue in six countries

Reading the Claude reviews, one complaint kept coming back: adults locked out by age checks. I
counted 12 of the 339 critical Claude reviews, from France, Korea, India, Japan, Brazil and
Germany, in six languages. None of the ChatGPT or Gemini reviews mention it.

- France: *"j'ai 19 ans et mes deux comptes on était bloqué pour être suspecté d'être utilisé par
  un utilisateur mineur"* (I'm 19 and both my accounts were blocked, suspected of being used by a
  minor).
- Korea: *"27살에 클로드 사용 중인데요 갑자기 미성년자 의심된다고 계정 잠금 당하고"* (I'm 27, and my account
  was suddenly locked as a suspected minor).
- India: *"My account is on hold for more than 4 months. I am 18+ still it's not working."*

This is exactly what a feedback pipeline should surface on its own, and obsei didn't. The model
filed these reviews under account, limits and refusals, and the themes kept them apart by
language. I found the pattern by reading.

## How far to trust the labels

The star ratings make a rough check possible. Treating 1–2 stars as negative and 4–5 as positive:

- **When the model said positive or negative, it agreed with the stars 96% of the time**
  (1,784 of 1,863 reviews).
- **It hedged a lot.** It called 427 of the 2,290 one-, two-, four- and five-star reviews neutral
  or mixed (19%).
- **Sarcasm fools it.** 9 one- and two-star reviews came out positive, including *"A box of rocks
  is more useful."* Going the other way, 70 four- and five-star reviews came out negative; many
  praise the app while complaining about one thing.
- **The area labels were right about four times in five.** I read 28 confident ones: 23 were
  right. Answers, pricing and account were the most reliable. Refusals and speech were the
  weakest, catching bans and comparisons that were about something else.

Stars aren't ground truth: people give five stars with a complaint and one star for things the app
can't fix. But a model that agrees with the stars this often, and says when it isn't sure, is
useful for sorting thousands of reviews. It isn't good enough to act on a single label unreviewed.
Run [`obsei eval`](https://docs.obsei.com/guides/classify/) on a sample of your own labelled feedback before routing on it.

## What didn't work

- **Short reviews grouped by language, not topic.** The largest theme, 165 reviews, is Korean
  reviews about everything: affection, limits, the age checks. Longer complaints with a clear
  topic did cross languages; one theme holds "wrong answers" reviews in Korean, Japanese, German
  and French.
- **Korean, Japanese and Chinese themes got no keyword labels.** The labeller skips words shorter
  than four characters, which drops most words in those languages. Other labels came out in the
  language of the reviews, such as *fausses* (French for "wrong").
- **No language detection.** The App Store feed gives a country, not a language, and the local
  classifier doesn't detect one, so these reviews have no language in Studio.

Each of these is now an issue for obsei 1.x. The age-check pattern is the test: next time, obsei
should find it before I do.

## Reproduce it

```bash
mkdir ai-apps && cd ai-apps
uvx --from "obsei[recommended]>=1.1" obsei init
# replace llms and pipelines in obsei.yaml with the ones below
obsei try               # check your questions on a few records first
obsei run && obsei themes
obsei studio --out studio   # a static, read-only Studio to open in a browser
```

```yaml
llms:
  default: {api: local, model: MoritzLaurer/bge-m3-zeroshot-v2.0}
pipelines:
  - name: ai-apps
    sources:
      - {key: chatgpt, type: appstore, config: {app_id: "6448311069", countries: [us, gb, in, br, de, fr, jp, kr], max_pages: 2}}
      - {key: claude, type: appstore, config: {app_id: "6473753684", countries: [us, gb, in, br, de, fr, jp, kr], max_pages: 2}}
      - {key: gemini, type: appstore, config: {app_id: "6477489729", countries: [us, gb, in, br, de, fr, jp, kr], max_pages: 2}}
    enrichers:
      - type: classify
        config:
          llm: default
          threshold: 0.4
          intents:
            bug: something is broken, crashes or shows errors
            feature_request: asks for a new feature or improvement
            complaint: unhappy, without a specific bug or request
            praise: likes or recommends the app
          fields:
            area:
              description: What is the review mainly about?
              choices:
                answers: quality, accuracy or usefulness of the answers
                limits: usage limits or message caps
                pricing: subscription price, billing or refunds
                account: login, sign-up or account problems
                speech: talking to the app out loud, voice chat or dictation
                images: creating or reading images and files
                reliability: slowness, crashes, errors or not loading
                refusals: refusing requests, censorship or restrictions
                other: none of the above
    sinks:
      - {type: parquet, config: {directory: exports}}
```

My first version had `voice: voice mode or speech`, and the model put "you are smart" and "Love"
under it. Rewording an option is often the biggest fix: try your questions on a few records with
`obsei try` before a full run.
