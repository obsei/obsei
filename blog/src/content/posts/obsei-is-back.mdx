Obsei went quiet for almost three years. Today it is back, rebuilt from scratch, and it will stay
fully open source.

This post is the story of how we got here, why I think Voice of Customer matters even more in the
age of AI agents, and where obsei is going next.

## Where obsei started

Obsei began in December 2020 as a small open-source Python library ([first release on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/obsei/0.0.2/)). The idea was simple: pull
customer feedback from wherever it lives (app stores, Twitter, Reddit, support desks), run it
through AI models for sentiment and classification, and send the result wherever a team works.
That was before ChatGPT, when "AI" for most teams still meant training your own classifier.

Obsei is also how I learned AI. I started by contributing to other open-source projects, above all
[Haystack](https://github.com/deepset-ai/haystack), and sending a few patches to [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co)
libraries such as [Transformers](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers). Obsei grew out of that: transformers for sentiment and zero-shot classification, then
small models I trained myself for the RBI hackathon, then early retrieval-augmented generation,
all before most people had heard of a chatbot.

Girish Patel and I founded [Oraika Technologies](https://oraika.com) to build a company around it. In 2022 obsei was named a joint
runner-up in the [Reserve Bank of India](https://www.rbi.org.in)'s first global hackathon, HARBINGER 2021, under the problem
statement *"Social media analysis and monitoring tool for detection of digital payment fraud and
disruption"*. The jury picked 24 finalists from 363 proposals from India and 22 other countries.
RBI described obsei as an AI-powered, real-time social media analytics tool that understands
customer feedback in their native languages.

## Why we stopped

Recognition did not turn into a business. We tried open source, enterprise pilots, the
entertainment industry and a lot of cold outreach. None of it stuck. Looking back, obsei taught me
far more than AI: it was a full course in how to fail at sales, how to fail at marketing and how to
fail at fundraising. Every one of those lessons shaped how obsei 1.0 is built and run.

We closed Oraika and moved on:
I now lead technology at [Bluepencil](https://bluepencil.com) and am building [Tashi](https://tashi.in), an end-to-end SaaS
for the social sector.

## Why now

Even while nobody was working on it, obsei kept being used. The repository has more than
[1,400 stars and 170 forks](https://github.com/obsei/obsei), and the old 0.0.x package is still
downloaded from [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/obsei/) every month. People still need this, and an
unmaintained library that copies raw customer text around is not what they should be running in
2026.

The problem never went away. If anything, it got bigger:

- **Customers talk everywhere.** Reviews, tickets, calls, communities and surveys, in dozens of
  languages. No team reads it all.
- **Feedback is personal data.** Names, emails, phone numbers, order ids and national IDs sit inside
  almost every message. Copying all of it into yet another SaaS, or pasting it into a public
  chatbot, is a privacy risk most teams can no longer accept.
- **Agents need ground truth.** AI agents now write code, triage tickets and plan roadmaps. They
  should answer "what are customers saying?" from real, cited evidence, not from a guess.
- **Models finally fit on your own machines.** Small open models, and now *decision models* that
  answer in a fraction of a second on a CPU, make private analysis practical for any team.

So obsei 1.0 is not a polish of the old library. It is a new product built around one rule:
**understand customers without moving their personal data anywhere it shouldn't go.**

## What obsei 1.0 is

- **Private by design.** Personal data is redacted at ingest (national IDs for seven regions,
  checksum-validated where the format allows; names optional), authors become pseudonyms, the database is encrypted, and nothing
  leaves your network unless you allow it ([privacy controls](https://docs.obsei.com/privacy/overview/)). Air-gapped is the default.
- **Bring your own sources and models.** 20+ sources, from App Store to Zendesk to SQL, plus
  plugins. Any OpenAI-compatible model, from Ollama to Azure, and [decision models](https://docs.obsei.com/guides/models/#decision-models) such as Julia-1
  for fast labels with real probabilities.
- **Themes across languages.** The same issue raised in English, Japanese and Portuguese becomes
  one theme, with trends, near-duplicate detection and k-anonymous views: groups from fewer than k
  people are hidden.
- **Built for agents.** A read-only [MCP](https://modelcontextprotocol.io) server lets Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor and your own agents
  query feedback with redacted quotes and record ids, never identities.
- **Act on it.** Route urgent bugs to Jira, billing to Slack and unsure cases to a human, based on
  model confidence ([routing](https://docs.obsei.com/guides/routing/)).

## Why you might use it

A few situations where obsei earns its place. Each one links to a complete, tested example
configuration.

**You ship an app and a release goes wrong.** Reviews start piling up in six App Store countries
and Google Play, in five languages. Obsei groups "can't log in after the update" in English,
Spanish and Japanese into one rising theme, and posts it to Slack before the support queue
explodes. ([app reviews example](https://github.com/obsei/obsei/blob/master/examples/app-reviews-multi-country.yaml))

**You run support and every ticket lands in one queue.** A small decision model on a CPU reads each
Zendesk or Freshdesk ticket once and answers typed questions: which team, how urgent, is the
customer angry, do they want a refund. Urgent bugs go to Jira and on-call, billing goes to the
billing channel, and anything the model is unsure about goes to a human.
([routing example](https://github.com/obsei/obsei/blob/master/examples/decision-routing.yaml))

```yaml
route:
  - review: [review]                     # the model is unsure: a person decides
  - when: {classify.intent: {is: bug, min_confidence: 0.8}, classify.fields.urgency: {gte: today}}
    sinks: [jira, oncall]
  - default: [lake]                      # everything else: your warehouse
```

**You work in a regulated industry.** Banks, insurers, health and public services often cannot send
customer text to a third-party SaaS. Obsei redacts Aadhaar, PAN, CPF, IBANs, card numbers and more
at ingest, encrypts the store, and runs air-gapped with your own model. This is the setting obsei
grew up in: spotting payment-fraud chatter on social media for the RBI hackathon.
([air-gapped example](https://github.com/obsei/obsei/blob/master/examples/enterprise-air-gapped.yaml))

**You serve several markets.** One pipeline per region, each with its own intents, channel and
privacy rules, while themes still show the issues they share.
([India, Brazil and Japan example](https://github.com/obsei/obsei/blob/master/examples/regional-india-brazil-japan.yaml))

**You build or use AI agents.** Point Claude, Cursor or your own agent at obsei's MCP server and ask
"Why are Japanese users unhappy this week?". The answer comes back with redacted quotes and record
ids you can check, and never with a customer's identity. ([MCP guide](https://docs.obsei.com/guides/mcp/))

```bash
claude mcp add obsei -- obsei mcp
```

**You run a nonprofit or social program.** Surveys, field reports and helpline messages are
feedback too, often from vulnerable people. Obsei lets a small team see what beneficiaries are
saying, in their own language, without exposing who said it.
([surveys example](https://github.com/obsei/obsei/blob/master/examples/surveys-typeform.yaml))

**You maintain an open-source project.** Watch GitHub issues, Hacker News, Reddit and Bluesky for
what users actually struggle with, and turn recurring themes into issues.
([social listening example](https://github.com/obsei/obsei/blob/master/examples/social-listening.yaml))

All examples are on the [Examples page](https://docs.obsei.com/examples/).

You can try the [live demo](https://docs.obsei.com/demo/) or run it locally in five minutes with
the [quickstart](https://docs.obsei.com/quickstart/).

## Open source, for real

Obsei is [Apache-2.0](https://github.com/obsei/obsei/blob/master/LICENSE) and will stay that way. There is no company behind it, no hosted upsell and no
open-core split. I will work on it in my free time, in the open.

<Principles />

The goal is for obsei to outgrow me. I want it to become a project worth donating to a neutral
foundation such as [LF AI & Data](https://lfaidata.foundation) or the
[Apache Software Foundation](https://incubator.apache.org) (through its Incubator), so that the community, not a
single vendor, decides its future. That needs contributors, users and honest feedback (ironic for a
feedback tool, I know). The groundwork is already in place: an
[OpenSSF Scorecard](https://scorecard.dev/viewer/?uri=github.com/obsei/obsei), container images
signed with cosign, a
[security policy](https://github.com/obsei/obsei/blob/master/SECURITY.md) and a
[contributing guide](https://github.com/obsei/obsei/blob/master/CONTRIBUTING.md) with DCO sign-off.

## What's next

- A release candidate after real-world connector checks, then 1.0 (see the
  [roadmap](https://github.com/obsei/obsei/blob/master/ROADMAP.md)).
- A new post here every week: practical guides, analyses of how themes shift across companies and
  domains, and experiments with new models.

If customer feedback matters to your team, and privacy matters too, give obsei a try, [open an
issue](https://github.com/obsei/obsei/issues/new/choose), or [start a discussion](https://github.com/obsei/obsei/discussions). Obsei is back, and this time it is built to last.
